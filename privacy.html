Bloomdex

Privacy policy

Last updated 27 October 2026

Bloomdex is a flower-identification app for San Francisco. Everything it does happens on your phone. This page explains what the app uses, what it stores, and what it never does.

What the app uses

  • Camera. To photograph a flower so the app can identify it. The photo is analysed on the phone by a model bundled with the app.
  • Location, while you use the app. To record the neighbourhood where you found a flower. The app rounds your position to about 100 metres before storing it and never stores a more precise fix.
  • Photo library, only when you choose a photo. To identify a flower from a picture you already took, or to save your flower photos to an album you choose.

What the app stores on your phone

  • Your collection: which flowers you have found, when, and the neighbourhood.
  • Your settings: language, sort order and narration preferences.
  • A log of recent identification attempts. It stays on the phone and is removed when you delete the app.

What the app never does

  • It does not send photos, location or any other data off your phone.
  • It has no account, no sign-in, no advertising and no analytics.
  • It makes no network requests of its own. Narration packs for languages other than English are downloaded from Apple after install, the same way the app itself is. The only other way out of the app is the Wikipedia button on a flower's page, which opens Wikipedia in an in-app browser after you confirm.

Children

Bloomdex is rated 4+. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

Delete the app. Everything it stored goes with it.
